
**Project and Team**

Title: Food insecurity by selected demographic characteristics
Group: Group Project 1 - Megan K, Miriam N, Keerit G, Kyle F

**Audience and KT Purpose**

The intended audience for this project is federal public health staff examining trends in household food insecurity over the past seven years and how food security status varies across demographic groups. Using the prepared dataset, the project aims to provide a clear, descriptive overview of differences in household food insecurity based on selected demographic characteristics, including gender, race/ethnicity, and Indigenous identity. By highlighting disparities across these groups, the analysis seeks to inform discussions on health equity and support the development of more targeted, evidence-informed policies and interventions for underserved populations.

**Questions:**

Primary: How does household food insecurity status vary across selected demographic characteristics (gender, race/ethnicity, Indigenous identity)?

Secondary: How do patterns of household food insecurity across demographic groups vary by geographic region?


**Data and Planned Output:**

We will use the publicly available CSV dataset, Food insecurity by selected demographic characteristics, published by Statistics Canada through the Government of Canada's Open Data Portal. The planned outputs include a descriptive summary table comparing household food insecurity status across selected demographic characteristics, as well as a figure illustrating regional differences in household food insecurity across demographic groups. Findings will be presented in a short Quarto report and a Quarto dashboard-style page featuring an interactive demographic filter to support comparisons between population groups.

**Known Limitations**

The proposed analysis has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings:

1. Unweighted analysis: The proposed summaries are unweighted and may not be representative of the broader Canadian population, limiting the generalizability of the findings.

2. Limited demographic categories: Gender and racial or ethnic categories within the dataset may be broad or generalized

3. Small subgroup sample sizes: Some demographic groups may have small sample sizes, limiting the reliability of comparisons.

4. Inability to establish causation: As the analysis is descriptive, findings can identify patterns and disparities in household food insecurity but cannot establish causal relationships between demographic characteristics and food insecurity status.
